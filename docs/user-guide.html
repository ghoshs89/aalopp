For grown-ups

Setting up a device

Puzzle Quest is a website that installs like an app. It works on tablets, phones and laptops, and Aishik's progress follows him from one device to the next.

1. Sign in once per device

  1. Open aalopp.vercel.app.
  2. Tap Sign in with Google and choose a family Google account. Only the family's approved accounts can get in; anyone else sees "That Google account is not on the family list."
  3. You land on Who is playing? The device stays signed in, so Aishik never has to log in himself.

2. Put it on the home screen

DeviceHow to install
iPad or iPhoneIn Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. Open it from the new icon so it fills the screen.
Android phone or tabletIn Chrome, open the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
LaptopIn Chrome or Edge, use the install icon at the right end of the address bar, or the browser menu's Install option. A bookmark works too.

3. Decide whether the app talks

Reading aloud is off when the app is first opened. The 🔇 Voice off switch at the top right of every puzzle screen turns it on; it then shows 🔊 Voice on. Each device remembers its own setting, so a tablet can talk while a laptop stays quiet.

For the most natural voice, see Making the voice sound natural.

4. Know where the grown-up controls are

At the bottom of Who is playing? there is a small Grown-ups link. It asks for the family PIN, then opens the progress pages. It stays unlocked for 30 minutes, or until you tap Lock.

How to play

  1. Tap your race car 🏎️.
  2. Tap Let's go! 🚀
  3. Read the puzzle, or tap 🔊 to hear it.
  4. Think it through. You can draw, or use paper.
  5. Tap your answer.
  6. Tap a face to show how sure you are: 😟 not sure, 🙂 kind of sure, 😄 very sure.
  7. See how you did, then tap Next puzzle ➜

There is no clock and no hurry. Getting one wrong is how your brain grows. When you finish, go stretch like a kangaroo 🦘.

The buttons you will see

  • 🔇 Voice offTap to turn the talking on. When it says 🔊 Voice on, every puzzle and answer is read out loud. Tap again to make it quiet.
  • 🔊Reads this puzzle out loud once, even when the voice is off. On Spanish and French puzzles, 🔊 Hear it plays the word in that language.
  • ✏️ ScratchpadOpens a drawing space with a blue pen, a red pen, Undo and Clear. It works with a finger or a stylus. It clears for every new puzzle.
  • 📝 I'm using paperTap this when working it out on real paper.
  • 🎤 Say itOn some language puzzles, say the answer instead of tapping. The app picks the answer it heard, and you still tap a face to finish. Tapping always works too.
  • 🏠Goes back to the start. Everything answered so far is already saved.
  • ☁️ 2A number here means answers are waiting to upload. It clears by itself when the internet is back.

For grown-ups

A session, start to finish

Each time Aishik taps Let's go, the app builds a fresh set of 8 puzzles just for him, based on everything he has answered on every device so far. A session is short on purpose: it ends after 8 puzzles, or quietly after about 12 minutes, whichever comes first.

flowchart TD
  A["Who is playing? Tap 🏎️"] --> B["App picks 8 puzzles for today"]
  B --> C["Puzzle shown, read aloud if Voice is on"]
  C --> D["Work it out: think, scratchpad or paper"]
  D --> E["Tap an answer, or say it on language puzzles"]
  E --> F["Tap a face: how sure?"]
  F --> G{"Right?"}
  G -- "Yes" --> H["You got it! Explanation if wanted"]
  G -- "Not yet" --> I["Let's check it together: answer and explanation"]
  H --> J{"3 wrong in a row?"}
  I --> J
  J -- "Yes, once per session" --> K["Offer a break: Take a break or Keep going"]
  J -- "No" --> L{"8 puzzles or 12 minutes?"}
  K --> L
  L -- "Not yet" --> C
  L -- "Done" --> M["Great work! Stars and break time"]

What Aishik sees after answering

  • Right: "You got it! Nice careful thinking. 🎉" A How do we know? link shows the worked explanation if he wants it.
  • Not right: "Let's check it together. 🔍" The right answer and the explanation are shown, and read aloud when the voice is on. If he tapped 😄 very sure, it says "You felt really sure. Let's look at it together." His choice is outlined and the right answer is highlighted, but there is no penalty.
  • Find the Bug puzzles: the wrong step turns red and shows what it should have said.

Breaks

If he gets 3 in a row wrong, the app gently asks "Want a short break?" with Take a break 🦘 and Keep going ➜. It asks at most once per session and never forces a stop.

Saving and the internet

Every answer is saved on the device the moment he taps a face, then uploaded. If the Wi-Fi drops in the middle of a session, he can keep going; the answers upload when the connection returns (during a session the app retries every 30 seconds). Starting a new session does need the internet, because the app has to look at his history to choose puzzles.

For grown-ups

How puzzles are chosen

Nothing about his progress is stored as a score. Every time a session starts, the app replays all of his answers and works out where each skill stands. That is why a phone and a laptop always agree.

Where each skill stands

StatusWhat it means
Not startedNo answers yet on this skill.
LearningHe has tried it, but has not yet got 4 of his last 5 right on 2 different days.
Mastered4 of his last 5 answers right, with those right answers on at least 2 different days. One good day is not enough, on purpose.
Review dueA mastered skill that is due to come back so it stays fresh.

The review schedule

After a skill is mastered, it returns for a quick check at growing gaps. Each right review moves it to the next gap. Any wrong answer sends the skill back to Learning.

  • First review after 1 day
  • then 3 days
  • then 7 days
  • then every 14 days

How the 8 puzzles are picked

  1. Try again: up to 2 questions he got wrong on an earlier day. (Not the same day, so a miss has time to settle.)
  2. Reviews: up to 3 skills that are due for review.
  3. Stretch: up to 2 questions one level harder than his current level in that area. These come last in the session.
  4. Everyday skills: at least 3 questions on time, money, measuring, graphs, calendars or area. These were his weakest area on IXL, so they appear in every session.
  5. Everything else by priority: skills he is learning, then new skills whose earlier skills he has already started.

Two more rules keep sessions varied: no skill gets more than 2 questions in one session, and a question he got right will not come back for 14 days. Easier puzzles come first as a warm-up.

Levels

Questions are rated from level 1 (warm-up) to level 5 (hardest). Each of the six areas has its own working level, starting at 2 because his MAP and IXL results are well above the easiest puzzles. An area moves up a level once he has answered at least 3 questions at a level and got at least 2 out of 3 right.

What the app never does: it never times him, never rewards speed, and never locks anything away. Time taken is recorded only so grown-ups can see it.

For grown-ups

What questions to expect

The puzzles are in the style of Math Kangaroo and Bebras contests, plus grade 2 and 3 skills. Many are themed around race cars, soccer and chess. Every question was checked by someone solving it without seeing the answer before it was allowed into the app.

Measurement and data 79 questions

Clocks to the hour, half hour, quarter hour and 5 minutes; how much time has passed; US and Canadian coins; making change; rulers in inches and centimeters (including objects that do not start at 0); bar graphs, picture graphs and a calendar; area and perimeter.

"The bus does not start at 0. How long is it?"

Logic and computer science 98 questions

Patterns, ordering clues, grid puzzles, chess-board squares, robot arrows on a grid, secret codes, reversing, paths that follow a rule, if-then rules, sorting, shortest routes on a map, gears, counting all the ways, and Find the Bug.

"Five toy cars had a race. White finished in the middle..."

Geometry and spatial 42 questions

Hidden triangles and squares, symmetry and mirror letters, cube buildings seen from above, views and shadows, matchstick shapes, turns and flips, folding and hole-punching.

"Fold a paper in half twice. Punch 1 hole. How many holes?"

Reading and languages 42 questions

Short original stories to read, with questions about details and about what the story does not say outright. Spanish and French words, colors, counting and everyday phrases, with the words spoken in that language.

"Why were everyone's socks brown after the game?"

Operations and algebra 34 questions

Adding and subtracting with regrouping, missing numbers, balance puzzles, rows and columns, multiplication facts and word problems, sharing and grouping, leftovers.

"A van holds 5 children. 23 are going. How many vans?"

Numbers and place value 33 questions

Careful counting and tally marks, skip counting by 2s, 5s, 10s and 25s, odd and even, place value to 1,000, and halves, thirds and quarters.

"Use each card once: 4, 0, 7. What is the smallest 3-digit number?"

Pictures in the puzzles

Analog clocks, coins drawn to real relative sizes, rulers, bar and picture graphs, a monthly calendar, route maps with road lengths, number and picture grids, drawn shapes and folds, and emoji scenes. Bars named after colors are drawn in those colors, and picture rows shrink to fit a phone screen.

Find the Bug

These show a named child's solution, step by step, with one mistake built from errors real children make: forgetting to carry, taking the smaller digit from the bigger one, reading the minute hand's number as the minutes, measuring from the wrong end of a ruler, or writing 45 + 30 minutes as 3:75. Later steps may build on the mistake, so the question always asks for the first wrong step.

Reading and language puzzles

  • Reading: the story sits in a "Read this" box and is never read aloud, so he practises reading it himself. Only the question is read aloud.
  • Hear it: a Spanish or French word or phrase is spoken, and he picks its meaning or picture.
  • Say it: he names a picture, color, number or phrase in Spanish or French, by tapping or, where the device supports it, with 🎤 Say it.

For grown-ups

Helping at home

  • One session a day is plenty. Mastery needs right answers on 2 different days, so little and often beats one long sitting.
  • Praise the working-out, not the speed. The ✏️ and 📝 buttons exist so careful thinking gets noticed. The "Worked it out" number tracks it.
  • Treat wrong answers as the good part. "Let's check it together" is designed to be read with him. Ask him to explain the answer back.
  • Watch the faces. If "very sure" answers are often wrong, slow down together on those. If "not sure" answers are usually right, tell him he knows more than he thinks.
  • Take the break when it is offered. Three misses in a row usually means tired or stuck, not "not smart".
  • Sit nearby for the first few sessions, especially for Spanish and French, where the device's voice and microphone can be imperfect.

When something goes wrong

"That Google account is not on the family list"

The account is not on the approved list. Sign in with one of the family accounts. Adding a new account needs a change to the app's settings.

Nothing is read aloud

Reading aloud starts switched off. Tap 🔇 Voice off at the top right so it shows 🔊 Voice on. If it is on and still silent, check the volume, and on an iPhone or iPad the silent switch or Silent mode. Tapping 🔊 next to a puzzle always tries to read it.

Making the voice sound natural

The app uses the best voice installed on the device: it looks for voices marked Natural, Premium or Enhanced, and skips robotic novelty voices. How good it sounds depends on what the device has, so installing a better voice once makes a big difference.

  • iPad, iPhone or Mac: in Settings (System Settings on a Mac), open Accessibility, then Spoken Content (Read and Speak on newer Macs), then Voices, then English. Download a voice marked Premium or Enhanced, such as Ava, Zoe or Evan. Do the same under Spanish and French for those puzzles.
  • Laptop: Microsoft Edge offers "Online (Natural)" voices, which sound the most human. Chrome uses its Google voices.
  • Android: in Settings, search for text-to-speech, choose Speech Services by Google, and install a high-quality voice for English, Spanish and French.

Close and reopen the app after installing a voice so it can find it.

Spanish or French sounds like an English voice

The device has no voice installed for that language. Add one as described in Making the voice sound natural.

There is no 🎤 Say it button

It appears only on some Spanish and French puzzles, and only in browsers that support speech input (Chrome and Safari do; Firefox does not). Tapping the answer always works.

The 🎤 heard the wrong word

Children's speech is hard for these services. The app shows what it heard and never submits by itself, so he can try again or tap the answer.

A ☁️ number will not go away

The device is offline or the connection is weak. The answers are safe on the device. They upload automatically once it is back online while a puzzle session is open, and starting the next session sends them too.

The session will not start

Starting a session needs the internet. Check the connection and reopen the app. If it sends you to the sign-in page, sign in again with a family account.

The pen draws when his palm rests on the screen

Once a stylus has been used, the scratchpad ignores fingers so a resting palm does not draw. With only a finger, lift the palm off the screen or use paper.

The grown-ups page asks for the PIN again

It relocks after 30 minutes, or when someone taps Lock. This keeps curious hands out of the notes and settings.

For grown-ups

Your data

  • What is saved: each answer, whether it was right, the face tapped, time taken, read-aloud replays, whether the scratchpad or paper was used and the number of strokes, why the question was picked, the device's date, and grown-up notes.
  • What is never saved: scratchpad drawings, voice recordings, or anything typed outside the answers.
  • Who can see it: only the approved family Google accounts, and only behind the PIN for the grown-ups pages. The database is locked so the website's public key cannot read it.
  • Speech input: when 🎤 is used, the browser sends the audio to its own speech service (Google in Chrome, Apple in Safari) to turn it into words. The app keeps only the chosen answer.
  • Starting over: practice history can be cleared on request while keeping his profile and sign-ins; it was cleared once on September 13, 2026, with a backup kept on the family computer.
